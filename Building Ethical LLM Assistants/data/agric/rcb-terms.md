## Interest rates and how they are quoted
Rural and community banks (RCBs) in Ghana are required to quote interest on farm
loans as an Annual Percentage Rate (APR). APR includes the base interest rate plus
all mandatory fees, so it lets you compare the true cost across lenders on a like-for-like basis.
Be cautious of advertisements quoting a flat monthly rate: a quoted rate of 5% per month
is equivalent to roughly 60% APR, not 5%. Always ask for the APR and the total amount
repayable before signing. Under the Borrowers and Lenders Act 2020, the lender must
give you this information before you commit.

## Season-tied tenor and repay-at-harvest schedule
Most RCB farm-input loans are structured around the cropping season. The loan is
disbursed at planting time and repaid in one or two instalments after harvest, when
the farmer has income. A typical tenor is four to eight months for a single season.
Some RCBs offer a two-season tenor for perennial or second-season crops. Ask your
branch for the repayment schedule in writing so there is no ambiguity about the
due date.

## Collateral and guarantor expectations
For smallholder loans below certain thresholds (which vary by institution), many RCBs
accept group guarantees from a registered FBO or cooperative in place of physical
collateral. Above the threshold, a personal guarantor with verifiable income or
assets may be required. Some RCBs accept warehouse receipts or an outgrower offtake
agreement as security. GIRSAL-backed loans through an RCB often require no personal
collateral for the portion covered by the guarantee.

## Fees to expect
Common fees charged by RCBs include a loan processing or administration fee (usually
1 to 3 percent of the principal, charged upfront), an insurance fee (some banks
require crop or life insurance linked to the loan), and in some cases a passbook or
account maintenance fee. All mandatory fees must be included in the APR disclosure.
Ask for a written schedule of all fees before accepting the loan, and confirm whether
any fee is refundable if the application is declined.

## Early repayment and penalties
Most RCBs allow early repayment; some charge a small prepayment fee. Check the
loan contract for this clause. Late repayment typically attracts a penalty rate
on the overdue amount. If you expect difficulty repaying on time — for example
because of a poor harvest — contact the bank before the due date; some RCBs can
restructure or defer a payment in hardship cases.

## How to compare offers from different banks
Ask every bank for the same information: APR, total repayable amount, repayment
schedule, all fees, and the collateral or guarantor requirement. Do not sign any
agreement before reading and understanding these terms. If a term is unclear, ask
the loan officer to explain it, or consult your MoFA extension officer or a
cooperative manager for a second opinion.
