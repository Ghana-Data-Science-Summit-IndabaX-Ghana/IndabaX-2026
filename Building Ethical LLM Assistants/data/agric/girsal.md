## What GIRSAL is
GIRSAL — the Ghana Incentive-Based Risk-Sharing System for Agricultural Lending —
is a government-backed guarantee facility managed by the Bank of Ghana. It does not
lend money directly to farmers. Instead, it shares part of the loss a participating
financial institution would suffer if a farmer defaults. By reducing the lender's
downside risk, GIRSAL makes it easier and cheaper for banks to approve farm-input loans.
Think of GIRSAL as an insurance policy the bank buys, not a loan you receive.

## How the guarantee changes your eligibility and terms
Because the lender's risk is lower, a GIRSAL-backed loan can come with:
lower collateral requirements (the guarantee substitutes for some security),
a lower interest rate than an uncovered farm loan from the same bank,
and faster approval in some cases, because the bank's credit committee needs
less security documentation before signing off.
The guarantee covers a portion of the principal — typically 50 to 75 percent —
so the bank still has some exposure and will still assess your creditworthiness.
GIRSAL does not guarantee 100 percent of the loan, and it does not remove the
need for a basic eligibility check.

## Which lenders participate
GIRSAL works through licensed financial institutions — rural and community banks,
savings and loans companies, and some commercial banks that have signed participation
agreements with the Bank of Ghana. Your local rural or community bank is the most
common access point. Not every bank in Ghana is a GIRSAL partner; ask your branch
whether it can originate GIRSAL-backed agricultural loans.

## What GIRSAL does not do
GIRSAL does not pay your loan for you if you cannot repay. It compensates the bank
for part of its loss after a default is declared, but you remain personally liable
for the full loan amount. A GIRSAL guarantee does not protect your credit record —
default is still recorded — and it does not mean automatic approval. The lender
still decides whether to extend credit. If you are struggling to repay, contact
your lender's hardship desk early; GIRSAL is not an escalation contact for borrowers.

## How to access a GIRSAL-backed loan
Apply through a participating rural or community bank. Ask specifically whether
the bank uses GIRSAL for agricultural input loans. You do not apply to GIRSAL
directly — the bank arranges the guarantee on your behalf as part of its own
internal process. Bring the same documents as for any farm-input loan: proof of
farming activity, national ID, and evidence of FBO membership if available.

## Sources of more information
The GIRSAL website and Bank of Ghana publications carry the current list of
participating institutions and the guarantee coverage rates. Your district MoFA
extension officer can also direct you to nearby GIRSAL partner banks.
