## How inputs-on-credit works in outgrower schemes
An outgrower or aggregator scheme is an arrangement where a company or cooperative —
called the nucleus firm or aggregator — supplies seeds, fertiliser, and agro-chemicals
to contracted farmers on credit at the start of the season. The farmer agrees in advance
to sell a specified quantity of produce to the aggregator at harvest. The value of the
inputs supplied is deducted from the sale proceeds at offtake, effectively repaying
the input loan automatically without requiring a bank transaction. This model removes
the need for cash repayment mid-season.

## Deduction at offtake
At harvest, the aggregator weighs and grades the farmer's produce. The cost of inputs
supplied — and any associated service or coordination fees — is deducted from the
purchase price. The farmer receives the net amount. The deduction schedule should be
set out in the contract signed before the season starts. Make sure you understand
exactly what will be deducted and at what commodity price before you accept inputs.

## Obligations of the farmer
As an outgrower, you are typically obligated to: use the inputs supplied for the
contracted crop only, follow the recommended agronomic practices specified by the
aggregator, sell the agreed quantity of produce to the aggregator at harvest,
and report any crop failure or shortfall to the aggregator promptly. Diverting
inputs to other uses or side-selling to another buyer can result in the full
input cost becoming immediately repayable in cash and may disqualify you from
future scheme participation.

## Advantages of tied schemes
Outgrower schemes offer several benefits: no upfront cash outlay for inputs,
guaranteed offtake (the aggregator commits to buying your produce), technical
support from the scheme's extension staff, and in some cases better input prices
through bulk purchasing. For farmers with no bank account or credit history,
an outgrower scheme may be the only accessible form of input finance.

## Risks to consider
The main risks are price risk and dependency. The offtake price may be set before
the season at a fixed rate, which could be below market if prices rise sharply.
Read the contract carefully for whether the price is fixed or linked to market
rates. If the aggregator has financial difficulty or collapses, your produce
offtake is at risk. Diversifying your marketing (selling part of your crop outside
the scheme) may not be permitted under the contract. Understand all restrictions
before signing.

## How to find a scheme
MoFA district offices keep lists of registered outgrower and aggregator schemes
operating in your area. The Ghana Grains Council and commodity associations such
as the Ghana National Association of Farmers and Fishermen (GNAFF) can also
direct you to active schemes. Always verify that a scheme is registered with
MoFA before supplying your land and labour.
