## What a warehouse receipt is
A warehouse receipt is an official document issued by a certified storage facility
confirming that a specific quantity and grade of a commodity — such as maize, rice,
soya, or cowpea — is held in storage under your name. Because the stored crop has
a known market value, a lender can accept the receipt as collateral for a loan.
The Ghana Commodity Exchange (GCX) operates a network of certified warehouses and
the registry that records and verifies receipts electronically.

## Eligible commodities
The GCX warehouse receipt system currently covers major food crops including maize,
rice, soya beans, and cowpea. The eligible list may expand over time. Commodities
must meet GCX quality and moisture standards at the time of deposit; grain that
fails a quality check cannot be stored under the receipt system. Perishable produce
or non-standard smallholder crops are generally not eligible.

## How to use a receipt to borrow
After harvest, you deliver clean, graded grain to a GCX-certified warehouse and
receive a warehouse receipt. You then present that receipt to a participating bank
or financial institution, which advances a loan against a percentage of the grain's
current market value — typically 60 to 80 percent. The grain stays in storage while
the loan is outstanding. When you repay the loan, the receipt is returned and you
can sell or withdraw your grain. If you default, the lender has the right to sell
the stored grain to recover its money.

## Typical terms and costs
Loan tenors are usually short — three to six months, tied to the marketing season.
Interest rates vary by lender but are often lower than unsecured input loans because
the collateral is liquid and verifiable. Storage fees are charged by the warehouse
(typically a monthly fee per metric tonne) and must be paid regardless of whether
you borrow. Factor storage costs into your total cost of financing before deciding
whether this route is cheaper than other options.

## Risks to understand
Price movement is the main risk: if commodity prices fall sharply during the loan
period, the value of your stored grain may drop below the loan outstanding, leaving
you with a shortfall even after the grain is sold. Some warehouses charge additional
handling or insurance fees. If the grain is damaged in storage, the warehouse is
liable under the GCX rules, but disputes can take time to resolve. Understand the
full fee schedule before depositing.

## How to access the system
Contact a GCX-registered warehouse or ask your district MoFA extension officer for
the nearest certified site. You can also check the Ghana Commodity Exchange website
for the current list of certified warehouses and participating lenders. The GCX
hotline can guide you through quality requirements before you deliver your grain.
